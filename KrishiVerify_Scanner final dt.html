    How a check works

    • Signature: each QR carries the pack details plus an Ed25519 signature. A forged or edited code fails, with or without internet.
    • Registry, recall, expiry: the manufacturer must be on the registry, the batch not recalled, the date not passed. The registry is signed by one authority (the main person); a device accepts changes only if the signature matches the authority it trusts.
    • Scratch code and seal: a copied QR does not carry the secret under the scratch panel. A broken seal adds risk.
    • Scan history: the same pack scanned many times on this device adds a clone warning.
    • Seller risk (optional): a logistic model trained on counterfeit_products_clean.csv scores the listing. Treat it as a weak hint: the dataset is about online sellers and separates classes almost perfectly, so real accuracy will be lower.
    • Suitability: a decision tree trained on fertilizer_recommendation_clean.csv (10,000 rows, about 88% cross-validated accuracy) predicts the fertiliser your pH, N, P and K suggest. The dataset shows no crop effect, so crop is not asked.

    Limits: a valid result means "consistent with manufacturer records", not "authentic or safe". The scratch code is stored hashed inside the signed QR, so a determined attacker holding a QR could try to guess it; a server-side check (see the notebook) removes that. Scan history here is per device. Only manufacturers who adopt the system can be verified. Any other QR or barcode is matched against the product register you load (if any) and otherwise gets a basic safety read (link red flags, barcode check digit, GS1 batch and expiry) but is never shown as verified.
